## Building bridges between networks
More broadly Maps of Making aims to build protocols so that network become network real networks. Usually network of association are relatively vertical in the sense that some are members and they don't really know what's happening at the top. Communication within a network is often relatively difficult. Let's say a makerspace network makes a deal with a repair cafe network. Or with a wood shop network or car mechanic network. That is done at the centralised level. At the representative level. In the capital. Now how does that propagate back to the small village where there is a repair cafe and there is a maker space? How do they start working together? What are the protocols? How do you find them? How do you collaborate with them? How do you communicate with them? And how do the these collaboration educate other collaborations in the same country? Let's say people in a repair cafe and a fab lab in Brussels manage to work very well together. How do they propagate their learning so that in a small city in Spain They also learn from these lessons of how this Fab Lab in Brussels managed to work constructively with the repair cafe in Brussels.

## Individuals are at the core : handshakes & documentation

At maps of making we are also working on the individual level. While most of all the development involves groups and small scale companies, association and institutions, we see the individual as the core elements. What we're building is ways for recognition that we call handshake. People giving opinions. Constructive opinions. But also verified opinions, but also bringing facts, objective truth in form of documentation. When someone does something and document it, we want to make it visible so that they can be recognized and somehow rewarded for that. So that when they do document something, it does not end up in a drawer or in an archive that nobody ever reads. Making it much less motivating to document something because you're convinced it will never see the light of day and never be useful to anyone else than yourself
